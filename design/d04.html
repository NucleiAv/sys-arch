Design a notification system

Send the right message to the right person on the right channel, push, SMS or email, without duplicates, without spam, and without losing the important ones.

The ask

Many teams in a company want to notify users. Order shipped, password changed, a friend liked your photo, a weekly digest. Build one shared service that takes these requests and delivers them through push, SMS and email providers.

Pin down the requirements

What it must do

  • Send push, SMS and email notifications.
  • Users choose which notifications and channels they want.
  • Templates with variables, in many languages.
  • Scheduled sends and bulk campaigns.

How well it must do it

  • Critical messages like one time codes arrive in seconds.
  • No duplicates, no lost critical messages.
  • Absorb huge bursts, like a campaign to 50 million users.
  • Respect provider rate limits and user quiet hours.

Out of scope, say it out loud

  • Building our own SMS carrier or push network. We use providers like APNs, FCM, Twilio and SES.

Do the math

10 million push, 1 million SMS and 5 million emails a day, plus occasional campaigns to 50 million users.

Key point

The average is tiny and the bursts are huge. Queues in front of channel workers are the heart of this design, so bursts wait in line instead of breaking things.

The API

POST /v1/notifications
  {
    "user_id": "u42",
    "type": "order_shipped",
    "priority": "high",
    "data": { "order_id": "o9", "eta": "Friday" },
    "idempotency_key": "order_shipped:o9"
  }
  reply 202 Accepted { "notification_id": "n123" }

It returns 202 Accepted, meaning "queued", not "delivered". Delivery happens later.

The big picture

  1. Notification API validates, deduplicates with the idempotency key, and records the request.
  2. Preference and rules check drops unwanted channels and delays sends in quiet hours unless critical.
  3. Queues per channel and priority, like push high, push low, email, SMS.
  4. Channel workers render templates and call providers with rate limits, retries and fallbacks.
  5. Status tracking from provider callbacks, delivered, bounced, opened, written to the log.
  6. Scheduler releases scheduled and campaign sends into the queues gradually.

Deep dives

Priority lanes

Login codes and security alerts must never sit behind a marketing blast. Use separate high priority queues and dedicated workers.

Deduplication

Idempotency keys at the API, plus a unique key in the notification log per user, type and object, like order_shipped:o9, stop double sends from retries.

Rate limits

Providers cap how fast you can send. Workers use a token bucket per provider, lesson 3.3. Also cap how many notifications one user can get per hour to avoid spam.

Campaigns

Do not dump 50 million messages at once. The scheduler feeds them gradually, by time zone, so users get them at a sensible local hour.

Invalid tokens

Push providers report uninstalled apps. Delete those tokens, or you waste capacity and may be throttled.

Trade-offs you must name

ChoiceGood atCosts you
One queue for allSimple.Campaigns delay critical messages.
Queues per channel and priorityIsolation and fair speed.More queues and workers to run.
Single provider per channelSimple contracts.Their outage is your outage.
Multiple providers with failoverResilient.More integrations, harder to keep templates and stats consistent.

Security and abuse

Notifications can leak data and can be abused.

  • Do not put sensitive details in push or SMS text, since lock screens show them.
  • Protect against SMS pumping fraud, where attackers trigger texts to premium numbers. Rate limit and check numbers.
  • Sign webhooks and verify unsubscribe links, so nobody can unsubscribe someone else.
