Design a payment system

Take a customer's money, pay the seller, and keep every cent accounted for, even when networks drop and providers time out. Correctness first, speed second.

The ask

An online marketplace needs to charge buyers by card and later pay out sellers. Payments go through an external payment provider. Money must never be charged twice, lost or made up.

Pin down the requirements

What it must do

  • Charge a buyer for an order.
  • Pay sellers their share later.
  • Refunds.
  • A full history of every money movement.

How well it must do it

  • Never double charge, never lose a payment. Correctness over speed.
  • Every cent can be explained, audit ready.
  • Secure. Card numbers never touch our servers.

Out of scope, say it out loud

  • Building our own card network, fraud models in depth, currency exchange.

Do the math

1 million orders a day.

Key point

Low volume, very high stakes. Use a relational database with transactions, idempotency everywhere, and a double entry ledger.

The API

POST /v1/payments
  headers  Idempotency-Key: order_8812_attempt_1
  body     { order_id, amount: 4999, currency: "INR", payment_method_token }
  reply    { payment_id, status: "succeeded" | "pending" | "failed" }

POST /v1/payments/{id}/refunds { amount, idempotency_key } POST /webhooks/psp provider sends final status updates ```

Amounts are integers in the smallest unit, paise or cents. Never floating point.

The data model

TableHolds
paymentsid, order id, amount, currency, status, provider reference, idempotency key unique
ledger_entriesentry id, transaction id, account, debit or credit, amount, time
accountsbuyer funds in transit, seller balance, platform fees, provider receivable
outboxevents to publish, written in the same transaction

Double entry ledger

Every money movement is written as two or more entries that add up to zero. A 100 rupee purchase with a 10 rupee fee writes debit provider receivable 100, credit seller balance 90, credit platform fees 10. If the books ever do not sum to zero, something is wrong, and you know at once.

The big picture

  1. Payment service with a SQL database, every write in a transaction.
  2. Idempotency keys on every incoming request and every call to the provider.
  3. Provider integration via tokens, card data goes from the browser straight to the provider, keeping us out of most PCI scope.
  4. Webhook handler for final statuses, idempotent and verified by signature.
  5. Ledger with double entry bookkeeping, append only.
  6. Reconciliation jobs comparing our ledger with provider reports daily.
  7. Payout service pays sellers in batches from their ledger balance.

Deep dives

Unknown outcomes

Timeouts do not mean failure. Treat them as unknown, keep the payment pending, and resolve by retrying with the same idempotency key, querying the provider, or receiving a webhook. Never guess.

Why a ledger

Updating a balance column loses history and hides errors. An append only ledger where entries always sum to zero gives a full audit trail and makes bugs visible.

Exactly once effects

Delivery is at least once everywhere, client retries, queue redelivery, webhook resends. Idempotency keys with unique constraints turn that into exactly once effects.

Security

Use the provider's hosted fields or SDK so raw card numbers go to them, not to you. Store only tokens. Encrypt everything and log every action.

Consistency choice

This is a CP system. If the database is unavailable, refuse to take payments rather than take them without records.
