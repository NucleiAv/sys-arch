Design a social news feed

When you open the app, you see recent posts from people you follow, fast. The famous question is whether to build feeds when posts are written or when feeds are read.

The ask

Users follow others and see a feed of their posts, newest or most relevant first. Think of the home timeline of X, Instagram or Facebook. The feed must load in a blink even though it mixes posts from hundreds of people.

Pin down the requirements

What it must do

  • Publish a post with text and images.
  • See a home feed of posts from people you follow.
  • Scroll back through older posts.

How well it must do it

  • Feed loads in under 200 ms at p99.
  • A new post appears in followers' feeds within a few seconds. Eventual consistency is fine.
  • Very high availability for reading the feed.

Out of scope, say it out loud

  • Comments, likes, ads, the exact ranking model.

Do the math

300 million daily users. Each opens the feed 10 times a day. 1 in 10 users posts once a day. On average users follow 200 people.

Key point

Reads are 100 times more common than posts. So doing extra work at write time, to make reads cheap, is usually worth it. Except for celebrities.

The data model

Feeds store only post ids. The posts themselves are fetched in one batch, mostly from a post cache.

Watch it work

Press Next or Play. Each step lights up one part and moves the requests. Arrow keys move steps after you click the diagram.

The big picture

  1. Post service writes posts and media pointers, then publishes an event.
  2. Fan out workers push post ids into followers' feed lists in Redis, capped at about 500 entries.
  3. Users above a follower threshold, say 100 thousand, are celebrities and are not fanned out.
  4. Feed service reads the user's list, merges in recent celebrity posts, optionally ranks, then hydrates post ids into full posts from a post cache.
  5. Inactive users are skipped during fan out and their feed is rebuilt on demand when they return.

Deep dives

Push versus pull

Fan out on write, push, makes reads instant but costs a lot for users with many followers. Fan out on read, pull, gathers posts at read time, cheap writes but slow reads for users who follow many people. Real systems use a hybrid, push for normal users, pull for celebrities.

Ranking

Chronological is simple. Ranked feeds score candidate posts with signals like closeness to the author, post age and engagement, using a model. Ranking happens at read time over a few hundred candidates.

Pagination

Use a cursor, the last post id or score seen, never offsets. New posts arriving at the top must not shift later pages.

Cache memory

300 million users times 500 ids times 8 bytes is about 1.2 TB, spread over a Redis cluster. Skipping inactive users cuts it a lot.

Deleted and private posts

At hydration, drop posts that were deleted or are no longer visible to this reader. Do not rely on cleaning every feed list.

Trade-offs you must name

ChoiceGood atCosts you
Fan out on writeVery fast reads.Expensive writes, wasted work for inactive followers, celebrity problem.
Fan out on readCheap writes, always fresh.Slow reads that query many authors.
HybridFast reads and bounded writes.Two code paths to merge.
Ranked feedMore engaging.Complex, needs a model and data pipeline.

Security and abuse

News feeds face abuse at scale.

  • Fake accounts and bots try to game ranking. Detect them with rate limits and behaviour signals.
  • Respect privacy settings in the fan out. A post for friends must never reach the public feed or a cache keyed only by post id.
  • Moderate content with a mix of automated filters and human review.
