The Solutions Architect exam and how architects choose

The AWS Certified Solutions Architect Associate exam tests whether you can pick the right design for a short business story. Learn its four domains, the keywords that point to an answer, the common traps, and a simple method that works both in the exam and in real design reviews.

The idea in one minute

The AWS Certified Solutions Architect Associate exam, code SAA-C03, has 65 multiple choice and multiple response questions in 130 minutes. 50 are scored and 15 are unscored trial questions you cannot tell apart. Scores run from 100 to 1,000, and 720 passes. Check the official exam guide for the current version and price before you book.

It has four domains.

DomainWeight
Design secure architectures30 percent
Design resilient architectures26 percent
Design high performing architectures24 percent
Design cost optimized architectures20 percent

Most questions are a short story. A company has a need and a constraint, such as "least operational overhead", "most cost effective" or "without changing the application". Usually two answers work. The constraint picks the winner.

Think of it like this

It is like a riddle where every answer can reach the shop, but the riddle says "the cheapest way" or "the fastest way" or "without a car". Read the last line twice, because that is where the riddle hides the real question.

The phrase in a question that decides between working answers.
Operational overhead
The work to run something, such as patching, scaling and backups.
Distractor
A wrong answer designed to look right.

Common traps

  • An answer that works but uses more servers when "least overhead" was asked.
  • Multi AZ chosen for read scaling. It is for availability. Read replicas scale reads.
  • Security groups to deny a specific IP. They only allow. Use a NACL or WAF.
  • Glacier chosen when data is needed in milliseconds.
  • Long term access keys in code instead of a role.
  • NAT gateway for S3 traffic instead of a gateway endpoint.

A method that also works in real design reviews

  1. Restate the need and the one main constraint.
  2. List the must haves, such as security, availability target and data size.
  3. Remove answers that break a must have.
  4. From what is left, pick the one that best fits the constraint, usually the most managed that works.
  5. In real life, also write down what you give up, the trade off.

Study plan

  • Learn the core services in this track, levels 1 to 6.
  • Do hands on labs, such as Skill Builder, Cloud Quest and workshops, listed on the track page.
  • Take the official practice question set, then timed practice exams, and review every wrong answer until you know why it was wrong.
Key point

Find the constraint, remove what breaks the must haves, then choose the most managed option that fits. That wins most exam questions and most design reviews.

Real life scenarios

Why the exam leans on security

Design secure architectures is the largest domain, at 30 percent. This matches real life, where most cloud incidents come from identity and configuration mistakes.

From exam to review

Many teams use the same Well-Architected questions from the exam in real design reviews. The AWS Well-Architected Tool in the console records the answers and risks for each workload.

Passing is a start

Hiring managers often say the certificate gets an interview, and hands on stories get the job. Pair study with a real project, such as hosting a secure three tier app with infrastructure as code.

Trade-offs

ChoiceGood atCosts you
Most managed optionLess work and fewer mistakes.Less control, sometimes higher unit price.
Cheapest optionLower bill.Often more work or less resilience.
Most resilient optionSurvives more failures.Higher cost and complexity.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing an answer that works but ignores the constraint.
  • Reading only the first half of a long question.
  • Memorising answers instead of understanding why.
  • Studying without any hands on practice.
  • Not reading the official exam guide for the current version.

Security lens

Security questions often hide in other domains. When two answers both work, prefer the one with roles instead of keys, encryption on, private networking, least privilege and logging. Practise in your own Free Tier or sandbox account with budgets and MFA on, and delete resources after each lab.
