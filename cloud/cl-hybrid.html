Connecting networks, peering, Transit Gateway, VPN, Direct Connect and PrivateLink

Real companies have many VPCs, an office or a data center, and partners. Learn the ways to connect them, which ones scale, how to reach AWS services and other companies' services without the internet, and how to choose.

The idea in one minute

Once you have more than one network, you must connect them. AWS gives several tools, each for a different job.

  • VPC peering. A direct private link between two VPCs. Simple, but not transitive. If A peers with B and B with C, A cannot reach C through B.
  • Transit Gateway. A hub that many VPCs and VPNs attach to. It replaces a web of peerings with a hub and spokes.
  • Site to Site VPN. An encrypted tunnel over the internet from your office or data center to AWS. Quick to set up.
  • Direct Connect. A private, dedicated network line from your data center to AWS. More consistent speed and latency than the internet, but it takes weeks to set up and is not encrypted by default.
  • VPC endpoints. Private access from your VPC to AWS services, such as S3, without going through the internet or a NAT gateway.
  • PrivateLink. Private access to one specific service in another VPC or another company, without connecting whole networks.
Think of it like this

Peering is a private bridge between two neighbouring estates. Transit Gateway is a central bus station that every estate connects to. VPN is a secure tunnel through a busy public road. Direct Connect is your own private road. PrivateLink is a hatch in the wall that opens to one shop only, not the whole estate.

Words you need

VPC peering
A private connection between two VPCs. Not transitive.
Transit Gateway
A regional hub that connects many VPCs and on premises networks.
Site to Site VPN
Encrypted IPsec tunnels over the internet between your network and AWS.
Direct Connect
A dedicated private network connection from your premises to AWS.
Gateway endpoint
A free route table based endpoint for S3 and DynamoDB.
Interface endpoint
A network interface in your subnet that reaches an AWS or partner service privately, powered by PrivateLink.
PrivateLink
Exposes one service privately to other VPCs or accounts without connecting the networks.
Client VPN
A managed VPN for individual users' laptops.

How it works

Choosing a connection

NeedChoose

Large, steady, predictable traffic to a data centerDirect Connect, with VPN as backup

Offer or use one service across accounts without network accessPrivateLink

Facts that are easy to get wrong

  • Peering is not transitive and does not allow overlapping IP ranges.
  • A Site to Site VPN connection has two tunnels for redundancy. Configure both.
  • Direct Connect does not encrypt traffic by default. Add an IPsec VPN over it, or use MACsec on supported connections.
  • Gateway endpoints exist only for S3 and DynamoDB, and they have no hourly charge. Interface endpoints have hourly and data charges.
  • Endpoint policies can limit which buckets or actions are reachable through the endpoint, a useful guard against data being copied to an attacker's bucket.

PrivateLink versus peering

Peering joins two whole networks, so both sides must trust each other and avoid overlapping ranges. PrivateLink exposes one service behind a Network Load Balancer, and the consumer sees only an endpoint in its own subnet. It is the usual way to offer a SaaS service privately to customers.

Key point

Choose the narrowest connection that does the job. PrivateLink for one service, peering for a few VPCs, Transit Gateway for many.

Real life scenarios

The peering mesh

A company connected 20 VPCs with peering. That needs 190 connections for every pair to talk, each with its own routes. Moving to a Transit Gateway turned it into 20 attachments.

Data out through the wrong door

A security review found that private servers could copy data to any S3 bucket in the world through the NAT gateway. An S3 gateway endpoint with a policy that allows only the company's buckets, plus egress rules, closed that path.

SaaS over PrivateLink

Many SaaS vendors, such as observability and database providers, let customers connect over PrivateLink, so customer data never crosses the public internet and no network peering is needed.

Common mistakes

  • Expecting peering to be transitive.
  • Only configuring one of the two VPN tunnels.
  • Assuming Direct Connect is encrypted.
  • Paying NAT gateway charges for S3 traffic instead of using a free gateway endpoint.
  • Connecting whole networks when one service would do.

Security lens

Every connection is a path for attackers too. Segment with Transit Gateway route tables, so dev cannot reach prod. Use endpoint policies to stop data leaving to unknown buckets. Encrypt traffic over Direct Connect. Log VPN and Transit Gateway flows, and treat a connection to a partner as a trust boundary in your threat model.
