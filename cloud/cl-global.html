Regions, Availability Zones and edge locations

AWS runs data centers all over the world, grouped into regions and Availability Zones. Learn what each one is, how they fail, how to pick a region, and why "deploy in two zones" is the first rule of high availability.

The idea in one minute

AWS splits the world into regions, such as Mumbai ap-south-1 or Frankfurt eu-central-1. A region is a separate geographic area. Regions are isolated from each other, so a problem in one should not affect another.

Inside each region there are several Availability Zones, or AZs. An AZ is one or more data centers with their own power, cooling and network. AZs in a region are miles apart, so a fire or flood rarely hits two, but close enough to connect with very fast, low latency links. AWS says each region has at least three AZs.

Then there are many edge locations in cities around the world. They run services that must be close to users, such as the CloudFront content delivery network and Route 53 DNS.

Why it matters. If you run one server in one AZ and that AZ has an outage, your app is down. If you run copies in two or three AZs behind a load balancer, the app keeps working.

Think of it like this

A region is a city where AWS has buildings. Each Availability Zone is a different neighbourhood with its own power plant. If one neighbourhood loses power, the others keep working. Edge locations are small shops on street corners all over the world that hand out copies of popular things fast.

Words you need

Region
A separate geographic area with several Availability Zones, such as ap-south-1 in Mumbai.
Availability Zone
One or more data centers in a region with independent power, cooling and networking.
Edge location
A site in a city used by services like CloudFront and Route 53 to be close to users.
Local Zone
A small AWS site near a large city, for very low latency to users there.
Multi AZ
Running copies across two or more AZs in a region, so one AZ failure does not stop you.
Multi region
Running in two or more regions, for disaster recovery or global users.
Data residency
A rule that some data must stay in a certain country or region.
Regional service
A service that lives in one region, like most AWS services. Some, like IAM, are global.

How it works

How to choose a region

  1. Law and data residency. Some data must stay in a country. Check first.
  2. Latency to users. Closer is faster. Users in India get lower latency from Mumbai or Hyderabad than from Virginia.
  3. Services. New services arrive in some regions first. Check the service is available.
  4. Price. Prices differ between regions.

Where each service lives

ScopeExamples
GlobalIAM, Route 53, CloudFront, AWS Organizations
RegionalS3 buckets, Lambda, DynamoDB tables, VPCs
Per AZEC2 instances, EBS volumes, subnets

An EBS disk lives in one AZ. To move data to another AZ, you copy a snapshot. A subnet lives in one AZ, so a VPC spread over 3 AZs has at least 3 subnets of each kind.

Levels of resilience

DesignSurvivesCost
Single AZServer failures, if you have sparesLowest
Multi AZOne data center or AZ outageModerate
Multi regionA whole region outageHighest, and hardest to build

Most production apps should be at least multi AZ. Multi region is for the most critical systems, see level 8.

Data transfer is not free

Traffic between AZs and between regions is usually charged. Chatty services that call across AZs all day can add real cost. Design for it.

Key point

Deploy every production system in at least two AZs. It is the cheapest big step in reliability you can take.

Real life scenarios

US East 1 in December 2021

An AWS outage in the Northern Virginia region, us-east-1, caused problems for many large apps for hours. Teams that depended on one region, or on services whose control plane ran there, were hit. It is a common lesson in knowing your region dependencies.

S3 in us-east-1, 2017

In February 2017 a typo in a command during maintenance took down a large part of S3 in us-east-1 for about four hours. Many websites broke, including AWS's own status page images. AWS changed its tools to remove capacity more slowly.

Data residency in banking

Many countries require some banking data to stay inside the country. Banks choose regions in that country and block other regions with organization policies.

Trade-offs

ChoiceGood atCosts you
Single AZCheapest, simplest, no cross AZ traffic.An AZ outage takes you down.
Multi AZSurvives data center failures, standard for production.Some cross AZ cost and more resources.
Multi regionSurvives a regional outage, serves global users.Much harder data consistency, double cost, complex failover.

Common mistakes

  • Running production in one AZ.
  • Picking a region by habit, ignoring law and user location.
  • Forgetting that EBS volumes and subnets live in one AZ.
  • Ignoring cross AZ and cross region transfer costs.
  • Assuming a global service has no regional dependency.

Security lens

Turn off regions you do not use with organization policies, lesson 2.2. Attackers who steal keys often start servers in far away regions where nobody looks, for crypto mining. Enable security monitoring, such as GuardDuty and CloudTrail, in every enabled region, not only your main one.
