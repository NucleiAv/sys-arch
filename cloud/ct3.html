Tier 3. A private internal HR app, multi AZ with backup and restore

An internal HR app slows the company down if it fails, but people can wait a day. Design it simply and cheaply, multi AZ in one region with backups copied to a second region, while still protecting sensitive personal data and keeping it off the public internet.

The ask

A company with 8,000 employees needs an HR app for leave, payslips, appraisals and personal records. Only staff may use it, from company laptops. If it is down for a day, work is annoying but nothing stops. It holds very sensitive personal data, such as salaries, bank details and health leave notes. Design it as a tier 3 system.

Pin down the requirements

What it must do

  • Staff view payslips, apply for leave and update their details.
  • Managers approve leave and write appraisals.
  • HR uploads documents and runs reports.

How well it must do it

  • Availability 99.5 percent. RTO 24 hours. RPO 1 hour in the region, 4 hours after a full region loss.
  • Reachable only by signed in staff on managed devices. Never public.
  • Personal data protected under India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023.
  • Low running cost.

Out of scope, say it out loud

  • The payroll calculation engine, which is a separate vendor system.

Do the math

8,000 staff, about 400 at the busiest hour. The database is 60 GB. Documents in S3 total 2 TB and grow 50 GB a month. Backups keep 35 days of point in time recovery and 12 monthly copies.

Key point

The load is tiny. The real design work is private access, protecting personal data, and backups you can actually restore.

The API

GET  /me/payslips?year=2026        own records only
POST /me/leave      { "from": "2026-12-01", "to": "2026-12-03", "type": "casual" }
POST /leave/{id}/approve           manager of that employee only
GET  /reports/headcount            HR role only

Every request carries the user's identity from SSO. The app checks the user's role and relationship to the record on each call.

The big picture

  1. Private access only. AWS Verified Access, or the corporate network over Direct Connect and VPN, in front of an internal ALB. Access checks identity from the company IdP and device posture from the device management tool. No public endpoint.
  2. App. ECS on Fargate, two tasks in two AZs, scaled down at night and weekends. Or a small serverless setup with Lambda. Either is cheap at this load.
  3. Database. RDS PostgreSQL Multi AZ with automated backups and point in time recovery for 35 days.
  4. Documents. A private S3 bucket with SSE-KMS, Block Public Access, versioning, Object Lock for records that must be kept, and access only through the app.
  5. Backups. AWS Backup plans copy database backups every 4 hours to Hyderabad in a separate backup account with vault lock. A restore test every quarter.
  6. Authorization. Role and relationship checks in the app, such as "manager of this employee", on every request.
  7. Audit. App level audit log of every view and change to personal records, plus CloudTrail and S3 data events.

Deep dives

Why backup and restore is enough

The business accepts a day of outage. Backup and restore costs very little compared with a standby, and AZ failures are already covered by Multi AZ. The trade is a slower, manual recovery after a rare regional disaster.

Why RPO is better than RTO here

RDS point in time recovery gives an RPO of minutes within the region. After a full region loss, the RPO is the age of the last copied backup, so the team copies database backups every 4 hours.

Verified Access instead of a VPN

AWS Verified Access checks each request against identity and device posture, without a network VPN. It fits zero trust, and every access decision is logged.

Personal data duties

Collect only what HR needs, set retention per record type, let employees see their own data, log access, and plan for breach notice. India's DPDP Act, 2023 sets these duties for personal data.

Trade-offs you must name

ChoiceGood atCosts you
Backup and restoreLowest cost DR.Hours to recover from a region loss.

Security and abuse

  • No public endpoint. Verified Access with IdP and device checks.
  • Least privilege inside the app, checked on the server for every request, which stops one employee reading another's payslip by changing an id.
  • Encryption with customer managed keys. The key policy allows only the app role and the backup service.
  • Separate HR admin roles with just in time access and approval.
  • Macie on the documents bucket, alerts on bulk downloads.
  • Backups in a separate account with vault lock against ransomware.
  • Audit logs kept per the company's retention policy.
