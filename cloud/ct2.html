Tier 2. A business critical online store, multi AZ with a warm standby

An online store loses money every hour it is down, but a short outage in a rare regional disaster is acceptable. Design a classic, secure three tier web app on AWS, multi AZ in Mumbai, with a warm standby in Hyderabad, decoupled order processing, and an RTO of one hour.

The ask

A retailer sells online across India. If the store is down, sales stop and customers go elsewhere. The business accepts up to an hour of outage in a full regional disaster, and up to a few minutes of lost data. Design the store as a tier 2 system.

Pin down the requirements

What it must do

  • Browse products, search, cart, checkout and order history.
  • Take payment through an external payment gateway.
  • Send order confirmations and update stock.

How well it must do it

  • Availability 99.95 percent. RTO 1 hour. RPO 5 minutes.
  • Survive an AZ failure with no customer impact.
  • p95 page load under 1 second for product pages.
  • Peak of 200 orders a second during sales, 20 times normal.

Out of scope, say it out loud

  • Warehouse systems and the payment gateway itself.

Do the math

At normal load the store runs 40 app tasks in Mumbai. The warm standby runs at 25 percent of that. Each order record is about 2 KB, and there are 2 million orders a day at peak season.

Key point

The data is small and the spikes are large. Caching, a CDN and a queue in front of order processing matter more than a huge database.

The API

The big picture

  1. Edge. CloudFront with AWS WAF managed rules and rate limits, Shield Standard, static files from S3 through origin access control.
  2. Web and app tier. An internet facing ALB in public subnets, ECS on Fargate tasks in private subnets across three AZs, with auto scaling on request count.
  3. Data tier. Aurora PostgreSQL with replicas in other AZs, ElastiCache for sessions and hot data, OpenSearch for search, all in private data subnets.
  4. Decoupling. Checkout writes a pending order and an SQS message. Workers handle payment confirmation, stock and email. A dead letter queue catches failures.
  5. Warm standby. In Hyderabad, the same stack from the same infrastructure as code, at 25 percent size. Aurora Global Database replica, S3 replication, container images replicated in ECR.
  6. Failover. A runbook, tested twice a year. Promote the Aurora secondary, scale up the standby, switch Route 53 records. Target under 1 hour.
  7. Operations. CloudWatch dashboards and alarms on checkout errors and latency, X-Ray traces, AWS Backup for point in time recovery and a separate backup copy.

Deep dives

Why warm standby and not active active

The business accepts an hour of RTO. Warm standby gives that at about a quarter to a third of the cost of a second full region, and avoids multi writer data problems.

Why a queue in checkout

The payment gateway and warehouse can be slow or down. A queue lets checkout reply fast and lets workers retry safely. With idempotency keys, no order is created twice.

Cache safely

Product pages are cached at CloudFront for a short time and in ElastiCache. Prices and stock are checked again at checkout, so a stale cache never sells at the wrong price.

Testing the warm standby

A warm standby that is never used drifts. Deploy to it with every release, run synthetic checks against it all the time, and do a full failover drill twice a year.

Security and abuse

  • WAF managed rules, bot control on login and checkout, rate limits per IP.
  • No card data stored. The payment gateway's hosted page or SDK handles cards, keeping PCI scope small. Webhooks are signature checked.
  • Security groups chain tier to tier. The database accepts only the app tasks' security group.
  • Secrets in Secrets Manager with rotation. Task roles per service.
  • Encryption at rest with KMS for Aurora, S3, cache and queues. TLS everywhere.
  • Customer accounts with MFA options, and credential stuffing protection on login.
  • GuardDuty, Inspector on images, and CloudTrail in the foundation accounts.
