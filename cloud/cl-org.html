Many accounts, Organizations, SCPs and single sign on

Real companies do not run everything in one AWS account. They use many accounts as walls between teams and environments, manage them together, and let people sign in once. Learn AWS Organizations, service control policies, Control Tower, IAM Identity Center, and where Cognito fits.

The idea in one minute

An AWS account is a hard boundary. Resources and permissions in one account are separate from another unless you connect them. So companies use many accounts, for example one per team and environment, such as payments-prod and payments-dev. A mistake or a breach in dev cannot touch prod.

AWS Organizations groups accounts under one management account. You arrange them in organizational units, OUs, such as Security, Prod and Dev, and you get one combined bill.

Service control policies, SCPs, set guardrails on whole OUs. An SCP can say "no account in Prod may use regions outside India" or "nobody may turn off CloudTrail". Even an account's admin cannot break these rules. SCPs never grant permissions. They only set the maximum.

IAM Identity Center, which used to be called AWS SSO, lets people sign in once, often with the company's existing login such as Microsoft Entra ID or Okta, and pick which account and permission set they need. Access is temporary and in one place, so when someone leaves, you remove them once.

AWS Control Tower sets all of this up for you as a landing zone, with log and audit accounts and ready made controls.

Think of it like this

A company is a block of flats. Each team gets its own flat with its own lock, so a fire in one does not spread. The building manager has rules that every flat must follow, like "no candles". And every resident has one key card that opens only their own flat and the shared gym.

Words you need

AWS Organizations
A service to manage many AWS accounts together, with one bill and shared policies.
Management account
The account that owns the organization. Keep it for billing and organization tasks only.
Organizational unit
A folder of accounts, such as Prod or Dev, used to apply policies.
SCP
Service control policy. Sets the maximum permissions for accounts in an OU. It never grants.
Landing zone
A secure, multi account setup ready for teams to use.
AWS Control Tower
A service that builds and manages a landing zone with ready made controls.
IAM Identity Center
Single sign on for AWS accounts and apps, formerly called AWS SSO.
Permission set
A set of permissions in Identity Center that becomes a role in each assigned account.
Amazon Cognito
Sign in and user management for your own app's customers, not for your staff.

Useful SCP guardrails

{
  "Effect": "Deny",
  "Action": "*",
  "Resource": "*",
  "Condition": {"StringNotEquals": {"aws:RequestedRegion": ["ap-south-1", "ap-south-2"]}}
}

Real region SCPs must exempt global services such as IAM, so use AWS's published examples as a start. Other common guardrails. Deny leaving the organization. Deny stopping CloudTrail or GuardDuty. Deny root user actions in member accounts. Deny creating IAM users with access keys.

Staff sign in versus customer sign in

NeedService

Your app's customers signing in to your appAmazon Cognito user pools, or another customer identity service
Your app's customers getting temporary AWS access, such as uploading to S3Cognito identity pools

Control Tower

Control Tower creates the organization, a log archive account, an audit account, Identity Center, and a set of controls, preventive ones as SCPs and detective ones as AWS Config rules. New accounts come from an account factory with the baseline already applied.

Key point

Use accounts as walls, SCPs as rules nobody can break, and one sign in that you can revoke in one place.

Real life scenarios

Account per team at scale

Large AWS customers often run hundreds or thousands of accounts. Separate accounts limit blast radius and make the bill easy to split, and Organizations plus Control Tower keeps the rules the same everywhere.

The leaver who still had keys

A company found that a former contractor's IAM user still had working keys months after leaving. Moving staff to Identity Center connected to the company directory meant that disabling one account removed all AWS access.

Crypto miners in an unused region

An attacker with stolen keys started servers in a region the company never used. An SCP that denies unused regions would have blocked it outright.

Common mistakes

  • Running workloads in the management account.
  • Giving people IAM users in every account instead of single sign on.
  • SCPs written without testing, which break deployments.
  • Thinking an SCP grants permissions.
  • Using Cognito for staff or Identity Center for customers.

Security lens

The management account is the most powerful account you own. Keep it nearly empty, lock its root user, restrict who can sign in, and alert on any use. Send organization CloudTrail logs to a separate log archive account with Object Lock, and deny members from changing security services with SCPs.
