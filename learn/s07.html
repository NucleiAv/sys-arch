Security risk management

You can never remove all risk. You can measure it, rank it and decide what to do with it, on purpose and in writing. Learn the definitions, the common ways to score risk, and how a risk register drives decisions.

The idea in one minuteWatch it, step by stepWords you needHow it worksReal life scenariosTrade-offsCommon mistakesPractice. Check yourselfPractice. Interview questionsPractice deskGo deeper

The idea in one minute

Risk is the chance that a threat uses a vulnerability and causes harm to an asset, together with how bad that harm would be. A short way to say it is

risk = likelihood × impact

A risk with a high chance and a small harm, like spam, can rank below a risk with a small chance and a terrible harm, like a fake firmware update that overheats babies.

Risk management is a loop. Identify risks, analyse and score them, treat them, monitor them, and repeat. Treating a risk means one of four choices. Avoid it, mitigate it, transfer it or accept it. Each choice has a named risk owner, a person with the authority to decide.

Think of it like this

Crossing a quiet street has a small chance of harm, so you look both ways and go. Crossing a highway has a big chance of terrible harm, so you avoid it and use a bridge. You made a risk decision both times.

Words you need

Risk
The chance of a harmful event and how bad its result would be.
Likelihood
How probable it is that the event happens in a given time.
Impact
How bad the result would be. Harm to people, money, law, reputation.
Inherent risk
Risk before any controls.
Residual risk
Risk after controls are in place.
Risk appetite
How much risk an organization is willing to take to reach its goals.
Risk owner
The person who has the authority and the duty to decide how a risk is treated.
Risk register
A living list of risks with scores, treatments, owners and dates.
CVSS
Common Vulnerability Scoring System. Scores the severity of a single vulnerability from 0 to 10.
ALE
Annualized loss expectancy. The expected money lost per year from one risk.

How it works

Qualitative scoring with a matrix

Most teams score likelihood and impact from 1 to 5 and multiply.

ScoreBandUsual action
15 to 25HighMust treat before release. Leadership sees it.
8 to 14MediumTreat on a plan, with a date.
1 to 7LowAccept or treat when cheap.

It is quick and easy to discuss. Its weakness is that the numbers are opinions. Write down why you chose each score.

Quantitative scoring with money

  1. Asset value, AV. What the asset is worth.
  2. Exposure factor, EF. The share of the value lost in one event.
  3. Single loss expectancy, SLE = AV × EF.
  4. Annual rate of occurrence, ARO. How many times a year you expect it.
  5. Annualized loss expectancy, ALE = SLE × ARO.

If a control costs less per year than the ALE it removes, it pays for itself. The FAIR model is a more detailed way to do this with ranges instead of single numbers.

Scoring vulnerabilities is not the same as scoring risk

CVSS scores how severe a vulnerability is in general, for example 9.8 critical. It does not know your system. A critical bug in a feature you turned off is a small risk for you. Add context.

  • EPSS estimates the chance that a vulnerability will be exploited in the next 30 days.
  • CISA KEV is a list of vulnerabilities that attackers are already exploiting.
  • Your context. Is it reachable? What asset is behind it? Is it safety critical?

Frameworks you will meet

  • NIST SP 800-30 for doing risk assessments.
  • NIST SP 800-37, the Risk Management Framework, for US federal systems.
  • ISO 31000 for risk management in general, and ISO/IEC 27005 for information security risk.
  • ISO 14971 for risk in medical devices. Security risks that can hurt patients feed into this safety process.
Key point

Accepting a risk is a valid choice, but only when the right person accepts it, in writing, with a review date. An engineer quietly ignoring a bug is not risk acceptance.

Real life scenarios

Equifax, 2017

A critical flaw in a web framework was announced with a patch in March. Equifax did not patch one public system. Attackers got in within weeks and took data on about 147 million people. The risk was known and scored high, but the process to treat it failed.

Log4Shell, 2021

A flaw in a common Java logging library scored the top CVSS score of 10. Teams that knew where the library ran, from software lists, could rank their exposure in hours. Others spent weeks searching.

A risk accepted the right way

A device maker found that an old Bluetooth chip could leak the device's serial number. Impact was low and the fix needed new hardware. The product head accepted the risk in writing for one year, and the next model used a new chip.

Trade-offs

ChoiceGood atCosts you
Qualitative matrixFast, easy to explain.Subjective, hard to compare with money.
Quantitative, ALE or FAIRSpeaks the language of budgets.Needs data you often do not have.
CVSS aloneStandard and widely used.Ignores your context and exploit activity.
Accept a riskNo cost now.The harm stays possible. Needs an owner and a review date.

Common mistakes

  • Treating the CVSS score as the risk to your business.
  • Risk registers that nobody updates after the first workshop.
  • Engineers accepting risks they have no authority to accept.
  • No review date for accepted risks.
  • Forgetting safety impact when scoring device risks.
